To the Editor, Applied Mathematics Letters

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "The missing pressure traction in Scott-Vogelius-Nitsche methods on polygonal approximations of a curved wall" for consideration as a letter in Applied Mathematics Letters. It is joint work of Claudemir de Souza Cavalcante and Jaideep Sai Padhi.

Exactly divergence-free finite elements are attractive for incompressible flow, but they are sensitive to how a curved wall is approximated, and weak imposition of boundary conditions is a common remedy. The letter should therefore interest readers working on divergence-free discretisations, Nitsche's method and boundary approximation.

Gjerde and Scott (Adv. Comput. Sci. Eng., 2024) proposed imposing no-slip weakly, by Nitsche's method, for Scott-Vogelius elements on a polygon inscribed in a curved wall, and L. R. Scott asked whether the method attains the error h_Gamma^{3/2} + h_Omega^k for general Stokes data, and if not, why not. We show that for the printed method the answer is no whenever the pressure varies along the wall. Its Nitsche form has no pressure traction, and we prove that the resulting leak through the wall is the leading error, with sharp rates and constant 1. We prove that a mean-free traction term (transcribed from Frachon, Nilsson and Zahedi, whom we credit) attains the conjectured rate for general data, and that the exponent 3/2 is sharp, unconditionally for every polynomial degree k >= 4 (under only a mild bound on the wall edge length). We also show that a Nitsche penalty scaling with h/min|e| is sufficient, and necessary under a shape condition on a boundary triangle. A short numerical study supports each result.

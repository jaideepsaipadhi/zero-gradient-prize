To the Editor, Applied Mathematics Letters

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "The missing pressure traction in Scott-Vogelius-Nitsche methods on polygonal approximations of a curved wall" for consideration as a letter in Applied Mathematics Letters. It is joint work of Claudemir de Souza Cavalcante and Jaideep Sai Padhi.

Exactly divergence-free finite elements are attractive for incompressible flow, but they are sensitive to how a curved wall is approximated, and weak imposition of boundary conditions is a common remedy. The letter should therefore interest readers working on divergence-free discretisations, Nitsche's method and boundary approximation.

Gjerde and Scott (Adv. Comput. Sci. Eng., 2024) proposed imposing no-slip weakly, by Nitsche's method, for Scott-Vogelius elements on a polygon inscribed in a curved wall, and L. R. Scott asked whether the method attains the error h_Gamma^{3/2} + h_Omega^k for general Stokes data, and if not, why not. We show that for the printed method the answer is no whenever the pressure varies along the wall. Its Nitsche form has no pressure traction, and we prove that the resulting leak through the wall is the leading error, with sharp rates and constant 1. We prove that a mean-free traction term (transcribed from Frachon, Nilsson and Zahedi, whom we credit) attains the conjectured rate for general data, and that, under a condition on the wall edge length relative to the smallest boundary angle and for nonzero wall shear, the exponent 3/2 is sharp for every polynomial degree k >= 4. We also show that a Nitsche penalty scaling with h/min|e| is sufficient, and necessary under a shape condition on a boundary triangle. A short numerical study supports each result.
The letter also incorporates Cavalcante's analysis of Scott's constant-pressure benchmark, which attains h_Omega^k + h_Gamma^{3/2} for every fixed k >= 4 under a bounded penalty window, and for every fixed k >= 2 on barycentric Clough-Tocher refinements.

The letter is written for a general applied-mathematics readership: the introduction recalls the history (Scott-Vogelius 1985, the zero-gradient phenomenon at polygonal walls, Nitsche 1971, Gjerde-Scott 2024) and explains the leak and its cure in words before any estimate, and each result is preceded by a sentence giving its idea. A paragraph on related work states precisely what is new relative to Cavalcante's earlier zero-pressure analysis, to optimal-order isoparametric Scott-Vogelius methods (Neilan-Otus 2021; Chen-Liu 2026 in 3D), to the boundary-corrected method of Liu, Neilan and Otus (2023), to the zero-net-flux formulation of Frachon, Nilsson and Zahedi (2024), and to the Scott-Vogelius stability theory of Guzman and Scott (2019).

The question was posed by L. R. Scott as a prize problem (PPL 115). The authors intend to submit this work for it, and have declared this in the manuscript.

The letter states its results and proves the key steps. Longer proofs and extensions (Clough-Tocher refinements, general domains, Navier-Stokes, three dimensions) are in an extended preprint by J. S. Padhi (Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23044296); code and numerical outputs are at https://github.com/jaideepsaipadhi/zero-gradient-prize.

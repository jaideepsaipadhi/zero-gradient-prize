To the Editor, Applied Mathematics Letters

Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript "The missing pressure traction in Scott-Vogelius-Nitsche methods on polygonal approximations of a curved wall" for consideration as a letter in Applied Mathematics Letters.

Exactly divergence-free finite elements are attractive for incompressible flow, but they are sensitive to how a curved wall is approximated, and weak imposition of boundary conditions is a common remedy. The letter should therefore interest readers working on divergence-free discretisations, Nitsche's method and boundary approximation.

Gjerde and Scott (Adv. Comput. Sci. Eng., 2024) proposed imposing no-slip weakly, by Nitsche's method, for Scott-Vogelius elements on a polygon inscribed in a curved wall, and L. R. Scott asked whether the method attains the error h_Gamma^{3/2} + h_Omega^k for general Stokes data, and if not, why not. I show that for the printed method the answer is no whenever the pressure varies along the wall. Its Nitsche form has no pressure traction, and I prove that the resulting leak through the wall is the leading error, with sharp rates and constant 1. I prove that a mean-free traction term (transcribed from Frachon, Nilsson and Zahedi, whom I credit) attains the conjectured rate for general data. I also show that a Nitsche penalty scaling with h/min|e| is sufficient, and necessary under a shape condition on a boundary triangle. A short numerical study supports each result.

The question was posed by L. R. Scott as a prize problem (PPL 115). I intend to submit this work for it, and have declared this in the manuscript.

The letter states its results and proves the key steps. Longer proofs and extensions (Clough-Tocher refinements, general domains, Navier-Stokes, three dimensions) are in an extended preprint, to be posted as version 3 of a Zenodo record (DOI to be inserted: 10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXXX), which will also archive the code and data. If the extended version is later submitted to a journal, its overlap with this letter will be declared. This manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. The use of generative AI is declared in the manuscript.

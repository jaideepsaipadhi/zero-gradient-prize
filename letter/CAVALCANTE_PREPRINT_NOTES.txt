1. ABSTRACT (verbatim)
----------------------
"We prove an H^1 error estimate for the Scott-Vogelius-Nitsche approximation of a two-dimensional Stokes
benchmark with constant pressure. The physical no-slip circle is represented by an inscribed polygon, while the
outer boundary is fixed. The volume form uses the symmetric gradient, but the Nitsche terms use the componentwise
normal derivative. Their flux mismatch is (grad u)^T n. On each polygon edge, incompressibility and tangential
integration by parts express this mismatch in terms of the exact velocity trace; the endpoint terms vanish because
the vertices lie on the no-slip circle. The chordal trace is O(h_Gamma^2), giving a consistency defect of order
h_Gamma^{3/2} after an inverse trace estimate. A local divergence-preserving approximation, with explicitly
specified compatible outer data, then gives ||u - u_h||_{H^1(Omega_h)} <= C (h_Omega^k + h_Gamma^{3/2}). The result
holds for fixed k >= 4 on shape-regular mesh families without boundary singular vertices and with uniform control
of nearly singular vertices. It also holds for k >= 2 on admissible Clough-Tocher splits. The effective penalty
gamma = mu h_Gamma / h_Omega must remain above a coercivity threshold and bounded from above. A reproducible quartic
computation studies the penalty on four meshes, enforces all cubic divergence moments, and locates the
finest-mesh coercivity transition near mu = 60.3383."

2. SETTING AND HYPOTHESES
-------------------------
- Case treated: ONLY the zero-pressure (constant-pressure) benchmark, eq. (2):
    "u(x,y) = (1 - 1/(x^2+y^2)) (-y, x),  p = 0."   Stream function psi = log r - r^2/2.
  Remark 5.4: "For general Stokes data, additional pressure and extension residuals must be analyzed; the theorem
  makes no assertion about those terms."
- Domain: D a bounded polygon with B_{1+delta}(0) in D; Omega = D \ B_1(0); P_h an inscribed convex polygon;
  Omega_h = D \ P_h ("Thus Omega subset Omega_h").
- Mesh scales (4): h_Omega = max diam T, h_Gamma = max |e| on Gamma_h, "c0 h_Gamma <= |e| <= h_Gamma".
- (M1) "no vertex of dOmega_h is singular"; (M2) "Theta(z) >= Theta_0 > 0 at every nonsingular vertex, with
  Theta_0 independent of the mesh." "Interior singular vertices are allowed."
- Nitsche form (13): N_h(w,v) = a_h(w,v) - <d_n w, v> - <w, d_n v> + (mu/h_Omega) <w,v>, with
  a_h = (1/2) int Dw:Dv. "This is the componentwise-normal-derivative Nitsche form associated with the benchmark
  in [4]" ([4] = Gjerde-Scott 2024). Effective penalty (15): gamma = mu h_Gamma / h_Omega.
- Method (14): u_h in Z_h(g_h), N_h(u_h, z_h) = 0 for z_h in Z_h (divergence-free kernel formulation; "Only the
  outer trace is enforced strongly"). Outer datum (12): g_h = (J_h u)|_Sigma, where J_h (10) = degree-k Lagrange
  interpolant + edge-bubble flux correction (flux-compatible). NOTE: this is NOT the plain P_k interpolant g_I of
  our letter; it is a flux-compatible datum.

3. MAIN RESULTS (numbers as in the preprint)
--------------------------------------------
Lemma 2.1 (uniform Korn-Poincare): "For all sufficiently small h_Gamma there is a constant C_K, independent of the
  polygon, such that ||v||_{H^1(Omega_h)} <= C_K ||Dv||_{L^2(Omega_h)} ... for every v in H^1(Omega_h)^2 with
  v|_Sigma = 0."
Lemma 3.1 (Flux-compatible interpolation): J_h "preserves the normal integral on every mesh edge and the integral
  of divergence on every triangle", with |u - J_h u|_{H^m(T)} <= C h_T^{k+1-m} |u|_{H^{k+1}(omega_T)}, m = 0,1.
Lemma 4.1 (Inverse trace estimate).
Proposition 4.2 (Stability): "There is a mesh-independent gamma_0 > 0 such that, for gamma >= gamma_0,
  N_h(v_h, v_h) >= (1/4) ||v_h||^2_{N,h} (v_h in V_{h,Sigma})." Continuity (20) "does not require an upper bound on
  gamma."
Lemma 4.3 (Trace on the polygon): "||u||_{L^inf(e)} <= C|e|^2, ||u||_{L^2(Gamma_h)} <= C h_Gamma^2."
Lemma 4.4 (Tangential identity): "On a straight edge e with constant orthonormal directions n, t, suppose that u is
  smooth, divergence free, and zero at both endpoints. Then int_e ((grad u)^T n).v ds = int_e (u_t d_t v_n -
  u_n d_t v_t) ds for every smooth edge test function v." "...it places no restriction on vorticity."
Proposition 4.5 (Geometric consistency): "For the benchmark (2) and gamma > 0,
  |N_h(u, z_h)| <= C (1 + sqrt(gamma)) h_Gamma^{3/2} ||z_h||_{N,h}  (z_h in Z_h)."
Lemma 5.1 (Compatible approximation): "Assume (M1)-(M2), k >= 4, and 0 < gamma <= gamma_1 for a fixed gamma_1. If
  u in H^{k+1}(Omega_h)^2 is divergence free, then there is w_h in Z_h(g_h) ... such that ||u - w_h||_{H^1} +
  ||u - w_h||_{*,h} <= C h_Omega^k ||u||_{H^{k+1}(Omega_h)}." Uses "the homogeneous local correction R_h = Pi_2 in
  [2, Lemma 6 and Theorem 7]" ([2] = Eickmann-Guzman-Neilan-Scott-Tscherpel, local Fortin projection).

*** THEOREM 5.2 (main theorem), verbatim: ***
  "Let the domains and meshes satisfy the conditions of section 2, including (M1)-(M2), and fix k >= 4. Let u be
  the field (2), prescribe (12), and assume
        gamma_0 <= mu h_Gamma / h_Omega <= gamma_1,                                        (31)
  where gamma_0 is large enough for proposition 4.2 and gamma_1 < inf is fixed. For sufficiently fine meshes, (14)
  has a unique solution and
        ||u - u_h||_{H^1(Omega_h)} <= C (h_Omega^k + h_Gamma^{3/2}).                       (32)
  The constant is independent of the two mesh scales."

4. PENALTY THRESHOLD / NUMERICS (Section 6)
-------------------------------------------
- Mesh: D = (-2.5,2.5)^2, N in {8,12,16,20} inner edges, "N/4 equally spaced outer segments on each side of the
  square ... Radially project these vertices to the unit circle", m = N/4 layers, "Each quadrilateral between
  consecutive rings is split along the same forward diagonal." P4 velocities, "all ten discontinuous cubic
  divergence moments on each triangle".
- Coercivity measure (34): lambda_min(mu) = min over ker B_f of z^T A_mu z / z^T H z, with H the full H^1 norm;
  bisection. "These are floating-point eigenvalue results, not interval-arithmetic certificates."
- Result (35), N = 20 (finest) mesh:  "60.33821 < mu* < 60.33831,  19.06512 < gamma* < 19.06516."
  "The explicit safe sign separation in the main table remains 60 < mu* < 65; the finer interval reports the
  computed resolution."
- "The choice mu = 100 is coercive on all four tested meshes and gives 29.32 <= gamma <= 33.11. The errors decrease
  from approximately 0.509 to 0.123." Table 1: H^1 errors 5.0856e-1, 2.8642e-1, 1.7807e-1, 1.2348e-1 for
  N = 8,12,16,20 at mu = 100. "this short mesh sequence does not determine an asymptotic exponent."
- The introduction states that the study "does not establish a universal numerical threshold or an asymptotic
  rate from four data points."
- COMPARISON WITH OURS (not put in the letter draft): paper/sections/13_numerics.tex l.8 reports that our code gives
  mu* = 60.343 at N = 20 for this benchmark. That lies about 0.005 above his bracket (relative 8e-5). Our criterion
  is the inertia of the velocity block plus a divergence penalty. His is the kernel-restricted generalized eigenvalue
  with the H^1 norm. The meshes may also differ (diagonal direction). Both criteria should give the same zero, so
  the gap probably comes from the mesh or from the bisection tolerance. Reconcile it before either number is
  quoted next to the other.

5. METHOD / PROOF IDEA (summary)
--------------------------------
(i) Uniform Korn on the polygonal domains via a radial bi-Lipschitz map F_h with ||DF_h - I|| = O(h_Gamma)
    (Lemma 2.1).
(ii) Standard Nitsche stability for gamma >= gamma_0 (Prop. 4.2).
(iii) Consistency (Prop. 4.5): Green's formula gives N_h(u,z) = <(grad u)^T n, z> - <u, d_n z> + (gamma/h_Gamma)<u,z>.
     The flux mismatch (grad u)^T n is integrated by parts along each chord (Lemma 4.4, using div u = 0 and
     u = 0 at the chord endpoints, which lie on the circle), so it costs ||u||_{L^2(Gamma_h)} ||d_t z||. Then
     ||u||_{L^2(Gamma_h)} = O(h_Gamma^2) (Lemma 4.3) and the inverse trace estimate give h_Gamma^{3/2}; the
     penalty term gives sqrt(gamma) h_Gamma^{3/2}.
(iv) Approximation (Lemma 5.1): a flux-compatible interpolant J_h plus the local Fortin correction of
     Eickmann-Guzman-Neilan-Scott-Tscherpel gives a divergence-free comparison function with the imposed outer
     trace and error h_Omega^k. The upper penalty bound gamma_1 is used here.
(v) Theorem 5.2 by coercivity on Z_h + (iii) + (iv).
Note: his Lemma 4.4 (the edge identity for (grad u)^T n) is the same mechanism our letter uses in Lemma 2
("normal-load cancellation": div v = 0 turns d_n v . n into a tangential derivative, then integrate by parts along
the edge). The difference is that he applies it to the exact u, and we apply it to the discrete test v.

I submit the manuscript "Scott-Vogelius elements on polygonal approximations of curved walls: the zero-gradient lock, Nitsche's method and the penalty threshold" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Scientific Computing.

Gjerde and Scott (2024) imposed no-slip weakly, by Nitsche's method, for Scott-Vogelius elements on a polygon inscribed in a curved wall, and L. R. Scott asked, as a prize problem (PPL 115), for a proof that the error behaves like h_Gamma^{3/2} + h_Omega^k, or an explanation of why not. The core answer for general data -- the printed method leaks through the wall at the rate (h/mu)(p - pbar) because its Nitsche form omits the pressure traction, and a mean-free traction (a device of Frachon, Nilsson and Zahedi, whom we credit) restores Scott's rate -- is announced, with proof sketches, in a short letter written jointly with C. de S. Cavalcante (see "Related manuscripts" below). The present paper is its companion and leads with material the letter does not contain: (a) when the zero-gradient lock of strongly imposed no-slip actually occurs -- under the Guzman-Scott angle condition strong imposition has the two-sided rate h_Gamma^{3/2} + h^k, and the lock comes from wall vertices lying in only two triangles, with a counting bound and a two-sided rate at locked vertices; (b) the penalty theory beyond a single boundary triangle -- necessity of the rho-scaling on every shape-regular mesh, and exact counterexamples showing that necessity cannot be read off a single wall star; (c) the exact H^1 constant of the leak, identified with the energy of a Stokes leak flow, and the leak limit uniformly in the penalty. It also gives complete proofs of the results that the letter only sketches, including the sharpness of h_Gamma^{3/2} for every k >= 4. Computations with up to 256 boundary chords confirm the rates and constants, and give practical advice on how to combine divergence-free elements with Nitsche's method.

The paper fits the journal's interest in the design and analysis of divergence-free discretizations, weak boundary conditions and boundary approximation; closely related work appeared in the journal, e.g. Durst and Neilan, J. Sci. Comput. 101 (2024), Paper No. 33, on divergence-free elements on smooth domains.

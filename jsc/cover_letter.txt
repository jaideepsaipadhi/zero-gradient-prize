I submit the manuscript "Exactly divergence-free Stokes elements with Nitsche's method on a polygonally approximated boundary: the missing pressure traction, Scott's rate, and the zero-gradient lock" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Scientific Computing.

Gjerde and Scott (2024) imposed no-slip weakly, by Nitsche's method, for Scott-Vogelius elements on a polygon inscribed in a curved wall, and L. R. Scott asked, as a prize problem (PPL 115), for a proof that the error behaves like h_Gamma^{3/2} + h_Omega^k, or an explanation of why not. The paper answers this in two dimensions. (i) For the method as printed, the estimate holds if and only if the wall pressure is constant: the Nitsche form omits the pressure traction, and the velocity leaks through the wall at the rate (h/mu)(p - pbar); we prove sharp rates for this error and identify its leading term. (ii) Restoring the traction with its wall mean removed (a device of Frachon, Nilsson and Zahedi, whom we credit) gives an exactly divergence-free method that attains h_Gamma^{3/2} + h_Omega^k for every k >= 4, and the term h_Gamma^{3/2} is sharp. (iii) The penalty must scale with the ratio of bulk to boundary mesh size. (iv) Strongly imposed no-slip attains the same rate under the Guzman-Scott angle condition; the zero-gradient lock comes from wall vertices lying in only two triangles. Computations with up to 256 boundary chords confirm the rates and constants, and give practical advice on how to combine divergence-free elements with Nitsche's method.

The paper fits the journal's interest in the design and analysis of divergence-free discretizations, weak boundary conditions and boundary approximation; closely related work appeared in the journal, e.g. Durst and Neilan, J. Sci. Comput. 101 (2024), Paper No. 33, on divergence-free elements on smooth domains.

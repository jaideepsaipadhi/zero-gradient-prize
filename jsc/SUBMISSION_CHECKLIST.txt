Scott-Vogelius elements on polygonal approximations of curved walls: the zero-gradient lock, Nitsche's method and the penalty threshold

ABSTRACT (plain text, no LaTeX; 232 words; limit 150-250):
Scott-Vogelius elements give pointwise divergence-free velocities, but when a curved no-slip wall is replaced by an inscribed polygon, strongly imposed no-slip can force a zero velocity gradient at polygon vertices, and the H^1 error can degrade to order h^(1/2). Gjerde and Scott therefore imposed the wall condition by Nitsche's method, and Scott asked whether the error is O(h_Γ^(3/2) + h_Ω^k) and, if not, why not. A letter written jointly with C. de S. Cavalcante announces the core answer with proof sketches: the printed method leaks through the wall at the rate (h/μ)(p - p̄) unless the wall pressure is constant, and a mean-free pressure traction restores Scott's rate. This companion paper contains what the letter does not. First, we determine when the zero-gradient lock occurs: under the Guzmán-Scott angle condition strongly imposed no-slip already has H^1 error ≍ h_Γ^(3/2) + h^k, and the lock comes from wall vertices lying in only two triangles, each costing h_Γ|∂_n u(z)|. Second, we give the penalty theory: the penalty must scale with the ratio of bulk to boundary mesh size, as shown by exact certificates on shape families and a proof on every shape-regular mesh, while exact counterexamples show that necessity is not local. Third, we identify the H^1 constant of the leak as the energy of a Stokes leak flow. Finally, we give complete proofs of the letter's results, including the sharpness of h_Γ^(3/2) for every k ≥ 4. Computations confirm the rates and constants.
(If the form rejects non-ASCII: replace Γ, Ω, μ, p̄, ≥, ≍, ∂_n, á by Gamma, Omega, mu, pbar, >=, ~, d_n, a.)

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection*{The question}
Scott--Vogelius elements, continuous piecewise-$P_k$ velocities with discontinuous piecewise-$P_{k-1}$ pressures, give discrete velocities that are divergence-free pointwise whenever $\div\Vh=\Pih$ \cite{ScottVogelius1985,GuzmanScott2019}. In three dimensions this holds on Alfeld (barycentric) refinements for $k\ge3$ \cite{Zhang2005,GuzmanNeilan2018}; see \cite{Neilan2020,FarrellMitchellScott2024} for surveys. At a curved no-slip wall, exact incompressibility has a price. If the wall is replaced by an inscribed polygon or polyhedron and the no-slip condition is imposed strongly, piecewise-polynomial divergence-free fields inherit gradient constraints at the wall vertices, and the $H^1$ error can lock at $O(h^{1/2})$ \cite{ScottPrize,GjerdeScott2024}. Gjerde and Scott \cite{GjerdeScott2024} keep the polygon and impose the wall condition weakly by Nitsche's method \cite{Nitsche1971}, with penalty $\mu/h$ and no pressure term. Scott's zero-gradient prize \cite{ScottPrize} asks for a proof that this Scott--Vogelius--Nitsche method converges, with the expected error $\hG^{3/2}+h^k$, and adds: \emph{if this does not hold, why not?}

The two-dimensional question is answered in a companion paper \cite{PadhiLong2D}. This paper treats the three-dimensional question: a smooth wall surface $\Gamma$, approximated by a closed triangulated surface $\Gh$ whose vertices lie on $\Gamma$, and a tetrahedral Scott--Vogelius mesh of the region bounded by $\Gh$ and a polyhedral outer boundary.

\subsection*{The methods}
The method as printed in \cite[(27)--(28)]{GjerdeScott2024}, which we call $\GS$, contains the symmetric Nitsche terms and the penalty $\mu/h$ but not the traction term $-\ip{p_h}{v\cdot n}$. Adding the plain traction makes the square system singular (Proposition~\ref{er:singular}). The corrected method $\CNS$ adds the traction with the wall mean of the discrete pressure removed, $\ip{p_h-\pbG(p_h)}{v\cdot n}$, to the momentum row only. It keeps the velocity exactly divergence-free. The device is not ours: the velocity of $\CNS$ coincides with that of the zero-net-flux formulation of Frachon, Nilsson and Zahedi \cite{FrachonNilssonZahedi2024}, transcribed to our setting \cite{PadhiLong2D}. What we add is its analysis on inscribed polyhedra.

\subsection*{Positioning}
The optimal-order alternative is to fit the curved boundary. Neilan and Otus proved optimal convergence of Scott--Vogelius elements on isoparametric curved triangulations in two dimensions \cite[Theorem~6.1]{NeilanOtus2021}. Chen and Liu \cite{ChenLiu2026} have recently extended this to three dimensions: isoparametric curved tetrahedral meshes, Piola-mapped Scott--Vogelius elements with $k\ge3$ on Alfeld splits, strongly imposed Dirichlet data and a commuting-interpolant load, with the optimal and pressure-robust rate $h^k$. Liu, Neilan and Otus \cite{LiuNeilanOtus2023} reach order $h^k$ in two dimensions on a mesh inside the curved domain, with a mean-free boundary multiplier, a zero-flux constraint and a Taylor boundary correction. Our setting is the cheap one that Scott's question is about: straight-sided, affinely mapped tetrahedra, an inscribed triangulated wall, no curved elements, multipliers or boundary corrections. Its best possible rate is $\hG^{3/2}+h^k$, and we show that this rate is attained and cannot be improved. We do not compete with \cite{ChenLiu2026} on rate. Our uniform inf-sup argument (Section~\ref{sec:stability}) follows the same route as \cite[Lemma~3.9]{ChenLiu2026}; see Remark~\ref{st:rem:chenliu}.

\subsection*{Main results}
Notation is fixed in Section~\ref{sec:setting}: $h$ is the bulk mesh size in Scott's penalty $\mu/h$, $\hG$ the largest diameter of a face of $\Gh$, $\rho:=h/\min_F\diam F$, $\gamma:=\mu\hG/h$, $\pbar$ the mean of $p$ over $\Gamma$ and $G:=\norm{p-\pbar}_{L^2(\Gamma)}$. Constants do not depend on $h,\hG,\mu,\gamma,\rho$. The solution $(u,p)$ is assumed smooth on $\overline\Omega$; at the edges and corners of the outer box this is a hypothesis on the solution, not a consequence of smooth data (Remark~\ref{set:rem:corner}). The domain is a general smooth obstacle problem (Assumption~\ref{set:dom}): the wall $\Gamma$ may have several components and need not be convex; the exterior of the unit ball in a box is the model case.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \emph{Geometry of inscribed surfaces} (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). On each face $F$ the distance to $\Gamma$ is, up to $O(\hG^3)$, the quadratic $\frac12\sum_{i<j}\hh(e_{ij},e_{ij})\mu_i\mu_j$ in the barycentrics of $F$, with the second fundamental form $\hh$ evaluated on the edge vectors (Lemma~\ref{geom:face}); on the sphere this is exact up to $O(\hG^4)$. The closest-point projection $\Gh\to\Gamma$ is a homeomorphism whenever the tetrahedral mesh is shape regular and the domain $\Oh$ does not reach the far side of the obstacle, condition (OB) (Theorems~\ref{geom:orient} and~\ref{geom:mesh}). Neither hypothesis can be dropped (Examples~\ref{ex:sliver} and~\ref{ex:filled}).
\item \emph{Stability} (Section~\ref{sec:stability}). The continuous inf-sup constant of $\Oh$ is uniform in $h$ (Lemma~\ref{st:bog}), and on Alfeld refinements with $k\ge3$ so is the discrete one (Lemma~\ref{st:IS3}), with an explicit dependence on the constant of a local result of Guzm\'an and Neilan \cite[Theorem~3.1]{GuzmanNeilan2018}.
\item \emph{The printed method leaks} (Theorems~\ref{er:A}, \ref{er:C}, \ref{er:B} and Corollary~\ref{er:Bp}). $\GS$ converges, with $\tnorm{\ut-u_h}\asymp(h/\mu)^{1/2}G$ and $\norm{\nabla(\ut-u_h)}\asymp h/\mu$ when $G>0$, and
\[
\ut-u_h=\frac h\mu\,v_\varphi+o\bigl((h/\mu)^{1/2}\bigr)\ \text{in energy},\qquad v_\varphi|_{\Gh}\approx-(p-\pbar)n,
\]
so that $\norm{\ut-u_h}_{L^2(\Gh)}/((h/\mu)G)\to1$ and $\tnorm{\ut-u_h}/((h/\mu)^{1/2}G)\to1$.
\item \emph{The leak limit and drag} (Section~\ref{sec:leak}). For every $\gamma\ge\gamma_0$, with no upper bound, $(\mu/h)(\ut-u_h)$ minus its geometric part converges in $H^1$ to the Stokes flow $U$ with Dirichlet data $-(p-\pbar)n$ on $\Gamma$, at rate $\hG^{1/2}$ (Theorem~\ref{lk:up}); on Alfeld refinements with $k\ge4$ the rate is exactly $\frac12$, with a lower bound weighted by the second fundamental form and an explicit constant (Theorem~\ref{lk:low}). So the $H^1$ constant of $\GS$ is $\norm{\nabla U}/G$, and $\norm{\nabla(\ut-u_h)}\le C(h/\mu+\hG^{3/2}+h^k+h^{k+1}\hG^{-1/2})$ uniformly in $\gamma$ (Corollary~\ref{lk:gen}). The drag, lift and torque of $\GS$ have error $-\frac h\mu K_\psi$ to leading order, with $K_\psi$ given by reciprocity (Theorem~\ref{dr:gs}); those of $\CNS$ converge at rate $2$ modulo (E$_Z$), a regularity assumption for the dual Stokes flow at the outer boundary (Theorem~\ref{dr:cns}).
\item \emph{The corrected method attains Scott's rate} (Theorem~\ref{er:D}). $\CNS$ is well posed for $\gamma\ge\gamma_2$, and for bounded $\gamma$ and $\hG\ge h^2$,
\[
\norm{\ut-u_h}_{H^1(\Oh)}\le C\bigl(\hG^{3/2}+h^k\bigr).
\]
On Alfeld refinements this holds for every $k\ge3$ with no further stability hypothesis.
\item \emph{Sharpness} (Theorems~\ref{sh:lb}, \ref{sh:alfeld}, \ref{sh:k3main} and Proposition~\ref{sh:bubble}). For $\CNS$, and for $\GS$ with any $G$,
\[
\norm{\nabla(\ut-u_h)}_{\Oh}\ \ge\ c\,\hG^{3/2}\,\bigl\|\,|\hh|\,\partial_Nu\bigr\|_{L^2(\Gamma)}-C\hG^2
\]
on Alfeld refinements for every $k\ge3$, and on every shape-regular mesh for $k\ge9$. For $k\ge4$ the proof reduces to an exact rank computation on one reference macro-element. For $k=3$ a single macro-element cannot work (Proposition~\ref{sh:k3single}); the witnesses live on the star of an interior edge, where the moment of a ``vertex move'' has a closed form (Theorem~\ref{k3:vertex}), and a Star Nondegeneracy Theorem (Theorem~\ref{k3:nondeg}) covers saddle and parabolic points.
\item \emph{Penalty} (Section~\ref{sec:penalty}). Coercivity holds for $\mu\ge C\rho$. Conversely, $N_h$ is indefinite on the divergence-free space if $\mu\diam F/h$ is below an exactly certified constant $\lambda_T>32$ of a single near-regular Alfeld macro-element (Theorem~\ref{pen:single}); $\lambda_T\ge20$ holds for every macro-element in an explicit box of shapes around the regular one (Proposition~\ref{ct:pen}); tall macro-elements have no single-cell witness, and a continuum witness with half-space constant $2327591/151008$ gives $\mu^\ast\ge c\rho$ for all shapes under local fineness (Theorem~\ref{pen:cont}).
\item \emph{Navier--Stokes} (Corollary~\ref{ns:cor}). Under the discrete linearised-stability conditions of \cite{PadhiLong2D}, which hold for small data and, for $h\le h_0$ (not explicit), on nonsingular solution branches, the error bounds of items~3 and~5 and the leak law carry over to steady Navier--Stokes flow, and so do the leak limit and the drag statements of item~4, with the Oseen leak flow in place of $U$ (Theorem~\ref{ns:oseen}).
\item \emph{Explicit constants} (Section~\ref{sec:constants}). On Alfeld refinements the witness constants are explicit functions of the macro-tetrahedron, from one certified reference norm; for regular macro-elements the constant of the leak lower bound is $5.20\cdot10^{-3}$ and that of the sharpness bound $2.37\cdot10^{-5}$. The discrete inf-sup constant is not explicit; we say why.
\item \emph{Worsey--Farin splits.} If the wall faces are subdivided, the discrete divergence is continuous along the interior edges of each wall face, so the inf-sup condition fails for the full discontinuous $P_{k-1}$ pressure space (Proposition~\ref{st:wf}), as is known from \cite{GuzmanLischkeNeilan2022,FabienGuzmanNeilanZytoon2022}. The sharpness witnesses exist for $k\ge4$ (Proposition~\ref{sh:wf}), but the upper bounds are not proved there.
\item \emph{Numerics} (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}). A three-dimensional Alfeld solver confirms pointwise incompressibility, the optimal rates $h^3$ and $h^4$ on a manufactured problem, a stable pair without spurious modes, and the leak law with constant tending to $1$ in slip and energy. At the mesh levels that fit our machine the sphere tests are pre-asymptotic for the $\hG^{3/2}$ rate; we give the commands for the finer levels.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{What changes from two dimensions}
Three things change. (i) In two dimensions the leading term of the tilt $n(x^\ast)-n_e$ of a chord is odd about the chord midpoint, which gives a dual-norm cancellation. In three dimensions its face mean is proportional to the offset between centroid and circumcentre (Lemma~\ref{geom:sag}), which is nonzero on non-equilateral faces. This turns out to be harmless for the rates: all upper bounds only need a penalty-weighted bound (Lemma~\ref{er:G}); the face means enter only constants (Remark~\ref{lk:rem:facemean}). (ii) Vertex terms of the two-dimensional normal-load cancellation become edge terms, which cancel to leading order because each edge of $\Gh$ lies in exactly two faces (Lemma~\ref{er:cancel}). (iii) Stream functions are unavailable. We replace them by a Piola--Hestenes extension of the closed $2$-form of the velocity (Lemma~\ref{geom:ext}) and by an interpolate-and-correct construction of the discrete leak field (Lemma~\ref{er:vphi}). The lower-bound argument is new in three dimensions: the sagitta is a quadratic form in the barycentrics, and the whole lower bound reduces to three edge moments of the normal derivative on each face.

Where a two-dimensional proof carries over with only notational changes we give the three-dimensional replacement of each ingredient and refer to \cite{PadhiLong2D} for the unchanged algebra; the three-dimensional arguments are given in full.

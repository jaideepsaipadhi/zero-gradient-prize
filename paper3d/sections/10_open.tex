\section{Conclusions and open problems}\label{sec:open}

\paragraph{Summary.} In three dimensions, as in two, the answer to Scott's question depends on the pressure term in the Nitsche form. The method as printed omits the pressure part of the wall traction, and when the wall pressure is not constant the discrete fluid leaks through the wall with normal velocity approximately $(h/\mu)(p-\pbar)$. This limits its convergence to order $h/\mu$ in $H^1$, with a constant that is the Dirichlet energy of the Stokes flow driven by the leak. Restoring the traction with the wall mean of the discrete pressure removed gives an exactly divergence-free method, $\CNS$, that attains the expected error $\hG^{3/2}+h^k$ on straight-sided tetrahedral meshes with an inscribed triangulated wall. On Alfeld refinements this holds unconditionally for every $k\ge3$, and the term $\hG^{3/2}$ cannot be improved wherever the wall is curved and the wall shear is nonzero. The three-dimensional arguments differ from the two-dimensional ones in where the curvature enters (through the second fundamental form and three edge moments per face), in a face-mean defect that affects constants but not rates, and in the replacement of stream functions by flux $2$-forms and discrete lifts. The method is therefore a cheap alternative to curved elements, at the price of the rate $\hG^{3/2}$ instead of $h^k$. Several questions remain open; we list them roughly in order of how much they restrict the scope of the results.

\paragraph{Meshes without splits.} The uniform discrete inf-sup condition (IS3) is, to our knowledge, open for the Scott--Vogelius pair on general unsplit tetrahedral meshes (Remark~\ref{st:rem:status}). All results that need existence of the discrete solution are therefore unconditional only on Alfeld refinements; on unsplit meshes they hold under (IS3). The lower bound of Section~\ref{sec:sharp} on unsplit meshes is proved only for $k\ge9$ (Proposition~\ref{sh:bubble}); for $4\le k\le8$ it is open.

\paragraph{Worsey--Farin splits.} On meshes whose wall faces are subdivided, (IS3) with the full space of discontinuous $P_{k-1}$ pressures is false (Proposition~\ref{st:wf}), as is known \cite{GuzmanLischkeNeilan2022,FabienGuzmanNeilanZytoon2022}. None of our upper bounds, and not the leak analysis of Section~\ref{sec:leak}, is proved on Worsey--Farin meshes. A proof would need three ingredients: a pressure space with the singular-edge conditions of \cite{FabienGuzmanNeilanZytoon2022}; an inf-sup constant for that pair that is uniform in $h$ on the perturbed domains $\Oh$, as Lemma~\ref{st:IS3} provides for Alfeld refinements; and a lift lemma onto the constrained trace space. The lower bound holds for every solution that exists, for $k\ge4$ on barycentric Worsey--Farin splits (Proposition~\ref{sh:wf}) and for $k\ge9$ in general. For $k=3$ there is no single-macro witness, the trace analysis of Appendix~\ref{app:k3} has not been redone for split faces, and the $k=3$ edge-star ranks are only numerical (Remark~\ref{sh:rem:wf}).

\paragraph{The penalty.} Coercivity needs a penalty $\mu\gtrsim\rho$ under local quasi-uniformity (LQ) at a smallest wall face, or when the cell at such a face has a certified single-cell constant. Necessity without either condition is open, as it is in two dimensions.

\paragraph{The leak and the drag.} The lower bound for the leak limit (Theorem~\ref{lk:low}) is proved only on Alfeld refinements with $k\ge4$; for $k=3$ there is no single-macro leak witness, and multi-macro witnesses would be needed. For general data at a fixed penalty, the lower bound of rate $\frac12$ is open (Remark~\ref{lk:rem:gen}). The rate $2$ for the drag of $\CNS$ (Theorem~\ref{dr:cns}) is proved modulo (E$_Z$), a regularity statement for the dual Stokes flow at the outer boundary $\Sigma$, and in the Oseen case modulo its analogue (E$_Z^\dagger$); whether the rate $2$ is sharp is not known. We do not treat the volume functional of Gjerde and Scott or pointwise tractions.

\paragraph{Explicit constants.} On Alfeld refinements with $k\ge4$ the witness constants are explicit (Appendix~\ref{sec:constants}). Three constants are not: the constant $\kappa_0$ of Theorem~\ref{k3:witness} (degree $3$ on general surfaces), which comes from compactness, although the sphere criterion of Theorem~\ref{k3:sphere} is explicit up to the right-inverse norm $C_E(\theta)$; the constants $\kappa_0,Y_0$ of Theorem~\ref{pen:cont}; and the (IS3) constant, because $C_\Pi$ and $C_B(\Omega)$ are not explicit and the local constant $\hat C_{\rm loc}$ has only been computed in floating point. The single-cell penalty bound $\lambda_T\ge20$ is certified on one explicit box of shapes, with the floating-point caveat stated in Appendix~\ref{sec:constants}, and not over larger classes.

\paragraph{Regularity at the outer boundary.} The smoothness of $(u,p)$ up to $\partial\Omega$ is a hypothesis on the solution: the edges and corners of the outer box limit the regularity that generic data produce (Remark~\ref{set:rem:corner}). Stokes $H^2$-regularity on $\Omega$, which an explicit threshold $h_0$ in Corollary~\ref{ns:cor}(ii) would need (Remark~\ref{ns:rem}), we can only reduce to a theorem on convex polyhedra attributed to \cite{Dauge1989}, whose statement we have not checked; (E$_Z$) rests on the same kind of input.

\paragraph{Computations.} The sphere computations we could run are pre-asymptotic. The rates $\hG^{3/2}$ of Theorems~\ref{er:D} and~\ref{sh:k3main}, the rate $\hG^{1/2}$ of the leak limit, and the separation of the rates of $\GS$ and $\CNS$ have not yet been observed in three dimensions (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}). The scripts for the finer levels are in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\paragraph{Sources checked indirectly.} We read the wording of \cite[Theorem~3.1]{GuzmanNeilan2018} through a text-extraction tool; \cite[Proposition~6.5]{Neilan2015} only through \cite{FarrellMitchellScott2024}; and the pressure spaces of \cite{GuzmanLischkeNeilan2022} only through its abstract and \cite{FabienGuzmanNeilanZytoon2022}. The Bogovski\u{\i} regularity used in Lemma~\ref{er:w} and the boundary Schauder theory for the leak flow in Section~\ref{sec:leak} are cited, not re-proved.

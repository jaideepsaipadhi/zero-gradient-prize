[1] 04_discrete.tex:65 and 12_three_d.tex:113, Galdi "Lemma III.3.4" (union of star-shaped
    pieces gives a uniform Bogovskii constant).  Also notes/v6/td_pen REPORT.tex:192.
    UNVERIFIABLE (no access to the book). No secondary source found citing a "Lemma III.3.4".
    Recommended REPLACEMENT for 2D (04_discrete): Bernardi-Costabel-Dauge-Girault, SIAM J. Math.
    Anal. 48 (2016) 1250-1271 [BCDG2016], Theorem 4.6 (author PDF costabel.perso.math.cnrs.fr/
    publis/BCDG_Conti.pdf, preprint layout). Stated there: for a curved Lipschitz polygon with
    piecewise C^2 boundary "there exists a constant c(Omega) such that for all h-approximation
    Omega_h of Omega |beta(Omega) - beta(Omega_h)| <= c(Omega) h". Definition 4.5(ii): "Its
    corners belong to the boundary dOmega of Omega", so the polygon is inscribed. Omega = R \ D fits.
    For 3D (12_three_d): the text right after Thm 4.6, which is not a numbered remark, says the same
    holds "for polyhedral approximations of three-dimensional domains"
    (regular triangulations of dOmega, diameters <= Ch, inner radii >= ch), but only "along the same
    lines of proof" (a sketch). Chen-Liu arXiv:2609.30008, Lemma 3.9 proof, uses [BCDG, Theorem 4.4]
    for this purpose. Thm 4.4 (verbatim): "if Omega and Omega~ are eps-close in Lipschitz norm ...
    |beta(Omega~) - beta(Omega)| <= c(d) eps" (Def. 4.1: diffeomorphism F with ||DF - Id||_inf,
    ||DF^{-1} - Id||_inf <= eps). NOTE: theorem numbers were read from the preprint layout. Check
    them against the SIAM version (HAL hal-01215296v3 is probably the final version).
    The explicit argument in the paper can stay; the citation can move from Galdi to BCDG.

[11] notes/v6/td_lift, Hildebrandt-Polthier-Wardetzky 2006.
    CONFIRMED (metadata + setting, secondary). Springer: Geom. Dedicata 123(1) 89-112 (2006), DOI
    10.1007/s10711-006-9109-5. The abstract assumes only Hausdorff convergence and does not mention
    normal graphs. Wardetzky's overview (convergence_cotan.pdf, "For a proof of the next theorem,
    see [15,29]"; [15] = HPW) gives Def 3.1 verbatim: "Mh is a normal graph over ... M if it is
    strictly within the reach of M and the map Psi: Mh -> M which takes every point on Mh to its
    closest point on M is a homeomorphism". Thms 3.3/3.4 assume normal graph. So the note's
    "normal-graph property is a hypothesis" is right. Cite the overview alongside if needed.

[12] td_lift, Morvan-Thibert 2004. CONFIRMED (metadata; definition via the same authors'
    follow-up). DCG 32(3) 383-400 (2004), DOI 10.1007/s00454-004-1096-4. The pages come from the
    reference list of Morvan-Thibert, "Unfolding of surfaces", DCG 36 (2006) 393-418. That paper,
    Def 2.2.2: "A triangulation T is closely inscribed in a smooth surface S if: 1. T is closely near
    S, 2. all the vertices of T belong to S". "Closely near": M lies in U_r(S), r = reach, and the
    restriction of the projection xi to M is one-to-one. This matches the note. The 2004 text itself
    was not read.

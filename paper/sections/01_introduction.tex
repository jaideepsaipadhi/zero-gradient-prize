\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Exactly divergence-free finite elements are sensitive to how a curved no-slip boundary is represented. On a polygon, a conforming, piecewise-smooth, divergence-free field that vanishes on the boundary satisfies extra homogeneous constraints on its gradient at the boundary vertices. When a curved wall is replaced by an inscribed polygon with more and more vertices, these constraints cause order-one gradient errors at the vertices. The $H^1$ error of strongly imposed Scott--Vogelius elements then decays only like $h^{1/2}$ \cite{ScottPrize,GjerdeScott2024}.

Gjerde and Scott \cite{GjerdeScott2024} impose the Dirichlet condition on the polygon weakly, by Nitsche's method \cite{Nitsche1971}, and observed $H^1$ errors of order $h^{3/2}$ on a benchmark with constant pressure. Scott's zero-gradient prize \cite{ScottPrize} asks for a proof that Scott--Vogelius--Nitsche converges for general Stokes data, including a computational study of the Nitsche penalty. It states the expected error $\hG^{3/2}+\hO^k$ and adds: \emph{if this does not hold, why not?}

\subsection*{Answer}
For the method as printed in \cite[(27)--(28)]{GjerdeScott2024}, the expected estimate holds if and only if the pressure is constant on the wall. The printed Nitsche form contains no traction term $-pn$. When $p$ varies along $\Gamma$, the penalty, not a traction, balances the pressure. The discrete velocity then leaks through the wall with normal velocity $(h/\mu)(p-\pbar)$, and the error is dominated by this leak (Theorems~A--C and Corollary~B$'$). A pressure-consistent correction restores the expected rate (Theorem~D), and the penalty threshold must grow with the ratio of bulk to boundary mesh size (Proposition~E).

\subsection*{Main results}
The notation is fixed in Section~\ref{sec:setting}. Throughout, $h=\hO$ is the bulk mesh size used in Scott's penalty $\mu/h$, $\hG$ is the boundary edge length, $\gamma=\mu\hG/h$ and $G=\norm{p-\pbar}_{L^2(\Gamma)}$. Constants do not depend on $h,\hG,\mu,\gamma$ or $\rho$.

\begin{theoremA}[Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
The printed method $\GS$ converges, and its error in the mesh-dependent energy norm is
\[
\tnorm{\ut-u_h}\;\asymp\;(h/\mu)^{1/2}\,G\;+\;O\bigl((1+\gamma^{1/2})\hG^{3/2}+\eps\bigr),
\]
with matching upper and lower bounds.
\end{theoremA}

\begin{theoremC}[Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
$\norm{\nabla(\ut-u_h)}\le C_p\,h/\mu+C\bigl((1+\gamma^{1/2})\hG^{3/2}+\eps\bigr)$.
The rate $1$ (rather than $1/2$) holds because $\div v=0$ turns the normal load into a tangential derivative (Lemma~\ref{lem:cancel}).
\end{theoremC}

\begin{theoremB}[Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
In the regime where the pressure term dominates, the slip satisfies $\norm{\ut-u_h}_{L^2(\Gh)}\asymp (h/\mu)G$ and $\norm{\nabla(\ut-u_h)}\asymp h/\mu$. So Theorem~C is sharp.
\end{theoremB}

\begin{corollaryBp}[Corollary~\ref{cor:Bp}]
$\ut-u_h=(h/\mu)\,v_\varphi+o\bigl((h/\mu)^{1/2}\bigr)$ in the energy norm, where $v_\varphi$ is an explicit divergence-free field with $v_\varphi\approx-(p-\pbar)n$ on $\Gh$. In particular
\[
\frac{\norm{\ut-u_h}_{L^2(\Gh)}}{(h/\mu)G}\to1,
\qquad
\frac{\tnorm{\ut-u_h}}{(h/\mu)^{1/2}G}\to1.
\]
\end{corollaryBp}

\begin{theoremD}[Theorem~\ref{thm:D}]
The naive consistent form, which adds $\ip{p_h}{v\cdot n}$, is singular (Proposition~\ref{prop:singular}). The corrected method $\CNS$, which adds $\ip{p_h-\pbG(p_h)}{v\cdot n}$, is well posed for $\gamma\ge\gamma_2$ and satisfies
\[
\norm{\ut-u_h}_{H^1(\Oh)}\le C\bigl(\hG^{3/2}+\hO^{k}\bigr)
\]
for general Stokes data, every fixed $k\ge4$ and bounded $\gamma$, provided the boundary edges are not too short relative to the bulk ($\hG\ge\hO^4$ for even $k$, $\hG\ge\hO^2$ for odd $k$). This is Scott's expected rate. Without that proviso a single lower-order term, due to the net flux of the interpolated outer data, remains.
\end{theoremD}

\begin{propositionE}[Proposition~\ref{prop:penalty}]
The coercivity threshold of the Nitsche penalty scales like $\rho=h/\min_{e\in\EG}|e|$. The condition $\mu\ge 4\kappa C_I^2\rho$ is sufficient. The condition $\mu\gtrsim\rho$ is necessary on meshes whose boundary stars are close to two reference stars, certified in exact rational arithmetic.
\end{propositionE}

Theorem~A with $G=0$ gives $\tnorm{\ut-u_h}\le C((1+\gamma^{1/2})\hG^{3/2}+\eps)$. This recovers, for the printed method, the constant-pressure benchmark studied numerically in \cite{GjerdeScott2024} and analysed in \cite{Cavalcante}, which covers $k\ge4$ and also Clough--Tocher splits with $k\ge2$. Our proof does not use \cite{Cavalcante}. All results hold for every fixed $k\ge4$; the computations use $k=4$.

\subsection*{Computations}
Section~\ref{sec:numerics} reports $k=4$ computations with up to $N=256$ boundary chords. They show the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item The $\CNS$ $H^1$ rate decreases monotonically to $3/2$, independently of the pressure.
\item The printed method has rate $1$ whenever $G>0$.
\item On a pure-pressure test that isolates the traction response exactly, the slip and energy constants of Corollary~B$'$ converge to $1$.
\item In the penalty study, the coercivity threshold $\mu^\ast$ is linear in $\rho$ over more than a decade, with $\mu^\ast/\rho\approx 18$--$21$.
\end{itemize}
All code, raw outputs and logs are in the accompanying repository (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

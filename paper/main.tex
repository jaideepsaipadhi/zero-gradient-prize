\title[Scott--Vogelius--Nitsche on polygonal boundaries]{Scott--Vogelius--Nitsche on a polygonally approximated boundary: convergence, the missing pressure traction, and the penalty threshold}
\author{Jaideep Sai Padhi}
\address{Department of Computer Science, Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN 47907, USA}
\email{jpadhi@purdue.edu}
\date{\today}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 65N30; Secondary 65N12, 65N15, 76D07}
\keywords{Stokes equations, Navier--Stokes equations, Scott--Vogelius elements, Nitsche's method, curved boundaries, divergence-free finite elements, Clough--Tocher splits}

\begin{abstract}
We analyse the Scott--Vogelius--Nitsche method of Gjerde and Scott for two-dimensional Stokes flow whose no-slip curve is replaced by an inscribed polygon: continuous $P_k$ velocities that are exactly divergence-free, with Dirichlet data imposed weakly on the polygon by Nitsche's method. This answers Scott's zero-gradient prize question, which asks whether the $H^1$ error behaves like $h_\Gamma^{3/2}+h_\Omega^k$ for general Stokes data and, if not, why not.

For the method as printed, the answer is no as soon as the pressure is not constant on the boundary. The printed Nitsche form omits the traction $-pn$. We prove that the method converges, at the sharp rate $h/\mu$ in the $H^1$ seminorm and at the exact rate $(h/\mu)^{1/2}$ in the mesh-dependent energy norm. To leading order the error is an explicit field: the discrete velocity leaks through the wall with normal velocity $(h/\mu)(p-\bar p)$, with constant exactly $1$ in the slip and energy norms. The $H^1$ rate is $1$ and not $1/2$ only because incompressibility turns the normal part of the missing traction into a tangential derivative. We also identify the $H^1$ constant: for fixed $\gamma$ and bounded $\rho$, $(\mu/h)$ times the error converges to the Stokes flow driven by the leak as Dirichlet data. This constant depends on the domain; it is $2.7565$ for our test and tends to $3/\sqrt7$ as the outer box grows.

The naive pressure-consistent correction is singular. A mean-corrected variant is well posed and attains $h_\Gamma^{3/2}+h_\Omega^k$ for general data, for every fixed $k\ge4$ on meshes satisfying (M0)--(M2) and every $k\ge2$ on Clough--Tocher refinements, provided the boundary edges are not too short relative to the bulk mesh. Whenever the wall shear is not identically zero, we prove a matching lower bound $c\,h_\Gamma^{3/2}$ for the $H^1$ error, so the $h_\Gamma^{3/2}$ term of Scott's rate is sharp. The lower bound is unconditional for $k\ge7$; for $4\le k\le6$ it is conditional on a local condition on the boundary stars, which we verified numerically on all our meshes but have not proved.

The Nitsche penalty must scale like $\rho=h_\Omega/\min_e|e|$. Sufficiency is proved; necessity is certified in exact rational arithmetic for every mesh whose shortest boundary edge carries a triangle with apex angle at least $35^\circ$ and base angles at least $5^\circ$. The convergence theory extends to general smooth curved boundaries in two dimensions (nonconvex and multiply connected, with a nonempty polygonal component carrying strongly imposed data), to steady Navier--Stokes under a linearised-stability assumption that we prove for small data, and to three dimensions for a spherical obstacle, under a uniform inf-sup assumption. That assumption is known on Alfeld splits for $k\ge3$, modulo the uniformity of the published constants over the perturbed domains, which we have not re-audited.

A computational study up to $N=256$ boundary chords, for $k=4,5,6$ and for Clough--Tocher refinements with $k=2,3$, confirms the rates and constants. It includes a pure-pressure test that isolates the traction response exactly, and a penalty study in which the threshold is linear in $h_\Omega/h_\Gamma$, with $\mu^\ast h_\Gamma/h_\Omega\approx20$ for $k=4$.
\end{abstract}
